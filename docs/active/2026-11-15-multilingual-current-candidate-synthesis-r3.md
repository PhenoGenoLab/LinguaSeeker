## Current conclusion

Seven newly audited or updated leads cover EIF2B5 leukodystrophy, FZD2-related omodysplasia, a Brazilian GLA/TTR/PRKAG2 cardiomyopathy report, and Chinese KDM3B, SOX10, SMPX, and CNKSR2 family cases. The KDM3B pair adds one verified within-article precision omission in the English translation (38+5 gestational weeks becomes 38 weeks), while its complete English translation carries the same patient, variant, family-testing, and clinical details. The SOX10, SMPX, and CNKSR2 reports have Chinese full texts and same-record English abstracts; their bodies include detailed clinical or pedigree facts that the abstracts omit. These are exploratory translation/detail findings, not confirmed new patient or variant acquisitions.

Current confirmed multilingual-only patient and variant increments remain **0**. A single precision omission in a convenience-discovered bilingual case does not estimate a population-average increment. The available cases cannot establish that multilingual acquisition is universally superior or even estimate an overall effect. Keep article, patient, family, variant, evidence-fact, translation-fidelity, and search-index outcomes separate.

## Evidence dimensions and accounting

Do not count article rows or extracted sentences as independent evidence. Review each target across:

- **Variant identity:** transcript accession/version, HGVS reference-base consistency, genomic assembly normalization, and database/literature correspondence.
- **Inheritance and family structure:** genotyped relatives, segregation, phase, and what tests were performed.
- **Phenotype and time course:** onset, system-level findings, imaging, biomarkers, measurements, treatment timing, and outcomes.
- **Source coverage:** original-language body, English abstract, same-record English full translation, and independent English publications.
- **Translation/source quality:** omitted operators or precision, contradictory terms, shared source errors, and indexing behavior.
- **Causal uncertainty:** distinguish author interpretation from measured findings; a computational model and one case do not prove variant causality.

For KDM3B, the English publisher page includes the same patient and variant; the 38+5-to-38-week omission is a precision difference within a same-article pair. It may be retained as a translation-fidelity event, but it cannot enter a confirmatory population estimate from a convenience sample. The reported variant is supported by its stated transcript reference base, but this does not independently validate the pathogenicity assertion. The article's “17 variants” total conflicts with its stated subtype counts (13+6+1+1=21); both languages repeat the same inconsistency, so it is not a translation effect.

For SOX10, SMPX, and CNKSR2, full text gives a finer clinical/pedigree record than the same-article English abstract, but no complete English-source correspondence audit is established for every body-only fact. The SMPX audit found prior English splice reports with similar maternal pedigrees; the CNKSR2 audit found likely duplicate patient rows from a prior English Xiangya report. These are fact-level candidates, not automatically new variants or independent patient/family units. Preserve the SOX10 father-phenotype conflict, SMPX pedigree-number conflict, CNKSR2 repeat-report risk, and ungenotyped relatives rather than counting them as clean segregation evidence.

## Generalization boundary

The current cases are convenience-discovered and do not share a probability-sampled denominator. They must not be pooled with earlier Chinese, French, Russian, Spanish, Rett/MECP2, or cross-gene observations as if every row were independent. Confirmed patient/variant increment of zero is a current evidence-gate status, not proof that the population effect is zero.

The current language search inspected only the first 25 returned records per language from exploratory Europe PMC queries (`LANG:chi`, 654 hits; `LANG:fre`, 386; `LANG:rus`, 72). Those are not eligible case counts or exhaustive searches. KDM3B exact-variant search finds its same bilingual record. SOX10 English-keyword search finds the Chinese record and prior English records for the same allele; its body facts still require the frozen full English correspondence audit. Access errors are missingness, never English-negative evidence.

An additional first-page Chinese lead is a homozygous germline `SH2B3 c.1481C>A (p.S494*)` MPN report (PMID 42161666). Its English abstract reports family genotyping and a ruxolitinib response, so those abstract-level facts are English-visible. The source body remains inaccessible in the examined routes (PMC reCAPTCHA, EPMC 403/500, DOI resolver 404). This is an unresolved access lead, not an English-negative or positive-increment case; see [SH2B3 access audit](2026-11-15-sh2b3-chinese-access-audit-r1.md).

To estimate a target-population average, freeze an enumerable, source-language-independent **question-conditioned** sample frame before candidate inspection, use paired frozen English and multilingual search procedures, specify the fact/patient/variant units and meaningful effect thresholds in advance, and power the sample using a defensible cluster-level variance estimate. The current 60-cluster design is only a coverage floor; it cannot establish a universal claim.
