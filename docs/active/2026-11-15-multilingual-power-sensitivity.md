## Purpose

The prior 60-cluster coverage floor is not a power-justified sample size. The user has since selected `δ=0.25` and simple random sampling from the finite Orphadata frame of 1,933 questions. This note shows how required sample size changes under provisional variance assumptions; it does not estimate variance from the exploratory cases, freeze the full protocol, or authorize confirmatory retrieval.

## Primary estimand used for the calculation

After the user selected simple random sampling, the planned primary estimand is the population-weighted mean of independently adjudicated, deduplicated, source-grounded fact units per sampled question. The fact unit must be present in verified non-English full text and absent from the frozen English correspondence audit. English workflow misses, patients, families, and relations remain separate secondary estimands. The weighting is implicit under simple random sampling, but the final analysis must still use the frame population as the target and preserve any nonresponse/missingness sensitivity rules.

## Illustrative calculation

For planning only, use the normal approximation for a one-sample mean with one-sided `α = 0.0125` and 80% power. The hypothesis is `H0: μ ≤ δ` versus `H1: μ > δ`, with the sensitivity alternative set to `μ=2δ=0.50`; thus the tested margin above the null boundary is 0.25. The alpha is an illustration of Bonferroni allocation across four primary/secondary endpoint families; the final multiplicity rule must be frozen before retrieval. This first calculation ignores finite-population correction.

\[
n = \left\lceil \left(\frac{(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{0.80})\sigma}{\mu_1-\delta}\right)^2 \right\rceil
\]

The resulting analyzed-cluster requirements are:

| `δ` with `μ₁=2δ` | `σ=1` | `σ=2` | `σ=3` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.25 fact units/question | 153 | 609 | 1,369 |
| 0.50 fact units/question | 39 | 153 | 343 |
| 1.00 fact unit/question | 10 | 39 | 86 |

With 60 analyzed questions, the corresponding approximate detectable margin above `δ` is 0.40, 0.80, and 1.19 fact units/question for `σ=1, 2, 3`. These are not estimates of current study variance; no confirmed primary outcomes exist yet.

## Finite-population simple-random sensitivity

Because the selected frame contains 1,933 questions and the selected allocation is simple random sampling without replacement, a second planning simulation applies the finite-population variance correction. It uses fixed synthetic count populations with mean `δ=0.25` under the boundary and mean `0.50` under the alternative, standard deviations `σ=1,2,3`, one-sided `α=0.0125`, 10,000 Monte Carlo repetitions per scenario, and a one-sided t lower bound. The synthetic populations are not estimated from exploratory cases.

The first tested grid sizes reaching at least 80% estimated power under the synthetic alternative were 150, 500, and 800 for `σ=1,2,3`; their Monte Carlo rejection rates were 0.860, 0.903, and 0.873 (10,000 repetitions each). At the current 60-question draft size, estimated power was 0.308, 0.012, and 0.0004 for those same scenarios. These numbers depend on the constructed zero-heavy finite populations and are not estimates from observed cases. Inflating the analyzed sizes to contacted sample sizes for assumed 10% or 20% MCAR unresolved targets gives 167/188, 556/625, and 889/1,000. This inflation addresses expected unusable-contact count only; it does not correct bias if unresolvedness is MNAR. The full machine-readable output, including finite-frame null calibration and Monte Carlo intervals, is [power_sensitivity_srs_finite_population_r1.json](../../benchmark/experiments/acmg_multilingual/overall_design/power_sensitivity_srs_finite_population_r1.json), generated by [simulate_multilingual_srs_power_r1.py](../../scripts/simulate_multilingual_srs_power_r1.py).

These figures are planning scenarios. They do not authorize retrieval, establish the true endpoint variance, or replace the required MNAR, source-universe, query-budget, multiplicity and stopping-rule freeze.

## Required freeze before confirmation

1. Choose the target population: the current candidate frame is 1,933 Orphadata disorders with an assessed germline association and HPO annotation. This is a bounded population and excludes unknown disease–gene association discovery.
2. The user-selected threshold is `δ=0.25`; preserve it without post-result changes. The alternative mean and variance distribution used for power planning remain assumptions and need a rationale separate from the threshold selection.
3. Select a defensible `σ` range using an external pilot or conservative assumptions; the existing convenience audits cannot supply an unbiased variance estimate.
4. Simulate the actual simple-random question estimator, zero-hit targets, nested patients/families/articles, unresolved English correspondence, and the missing-not-at-random sensitivity bounds. The current simulation is a transparent sensitivity check, not this final design-faithful analysis.
5. Freeze the maximum unresolved fraction overall and across access-relevant subgroups, multiplicity adjustment, stopping/futility rules, and the exact query budget for English and multilingual arms.

Until these inputs are frozen, the 60-cluster floor is a coverage target only. No superiority claim should use it as if it provided 80% power.
